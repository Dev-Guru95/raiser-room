Fundraising is rarely lost on the headline number.
It is lost on the second one.

Your pitch deck says revenue was 2.1 million. Your spreadsheet says 2.08. The data room has management accounts saying something different again, because they were exported three weeks earlier and nobody went back to update the deck.

Pitch deck, page 6$2,100,000
Financial model, tab “Summary”$2,080,000
Management accounts, Q4$2,064,300

None of those is a lie. Each was right the day it was written.

But an investor who spots two of them stops reading for the number and starts reading for the pattern. The question changes from how is this business doing to do these people know what is true about their own company — and that question is very expensive to answer once it has been asked.

Why it happens: numbers get copied. Worked out once, then typed into the deck, pasted into a spreadsheet, quoted in an email. Every copy ages on its own, and there is no single place to fix it.

How it works

Three things, in the order you do them.

See where you stand

Eight areas, thirty-two questions, and you can see exactly how the score was worked out. One weak area caps the result, because no company is “nearly ready” with a hole in it. You get back a list of what is missing, in the order that unblocks the most work soonest — with an owner and a date against each.

Put your numbers in once

Upload your accounts, contracts and cap table, and figures are read out of them and offered to you. Nothing becomes usable until you confirm it. Each number is then kept with where it came from, when, and who checked it — and if two numbers that should match do not, you are shown both, side by side, with neither one picked for you.

Build documents from them

Pitch decks, investor updates, one-pagers, your data room index. Each one points at your saved numbers rather than containing its own copy — so fix a figure once and everything using it is flagged to rebuild. Two documents built from the same record cannot disagree.

If a document needs a figure you have not recorded, it stops and tells you which one rather than filling the gap with something plausible.

What makes it different

Proof behind every number

Typed in, read from a document, confirmed by you, or calculated with its sum attached. Four ways in, and no fifth. There is deliberately no “the AI worked it out”.

An adviser that cites its sources

It answers from your records and a dated reference library, with every claim pointing at where it came from. It says so when it does not know, and it cannot write a number at all.

Share links that stay yours

One link for one named person, showing only what their stage allows, switched off for them alone whenever you want. You see when they opened it and for how long. IP addresses are deliberately not recorded.

Your data never meets another customer's

No benchmarks, no averages, no “companies like yours”, no market reports. Not a policy somebody enforces — the software has no way to read across companies.

It keeps working after the money lands

Lock a forecast, close a month, explain the differences in writing at the time. Two years later you can still show the number you reported and the document it came from — which is the one thing nobody can rebuild afterwards.

On cross-company data: automated tests fail if anybody adds a path that reads across organisations. It is a property of the build, not a review step somebody performs.

What RaiseRoom is not

Said here rather than discovered later.

It does not value your company.

It prepares you for a valuation and tells you what a valuer will ask you to evidence. It does not produce a valuation, an estimate, a range or an opinion of worth.

It does not raise money or introduce you to investors.

It is a tool for organising and evidencing what you already have.

It will not write your numbers.

Every figure in every document comes from one you recorded or confirmed. That is the product, not a limitation of it.

It is not legal, financial, tax or investment advice.

We are not your lawyer, accountant, broker or adviser.

It is not a replacement for your data room.

It is the layer that knows whether what is in one is consistent, current and enough.

Questions

Does the AI see my accounts?

No. The adviser is sent the question you typed and the relevant passages from a reference library held in version control. It is not sent your figures, your documents or your data room. Translation sends the passage with every number removed first, and a translation that comes back with a number in it is rejected.

Is my data used to train anything?

No. Your figures and documents are not used to train, fine-tune or evaluate any model, ours or anybody else's.

Can other companies see my data, even anonymised?

No. No figure, document, forecast, question or answer from your organisation is ever visible to another customer, and no benchmark, average or market report is ever computed across customers. The software has no code path that reads across companies.

What happens if I cancel?

Nothing is deleted. Your organisation becomes read-only, you keep reading everything, and you keep exporting everything in full.

Can I get my data out?

At any time, in one click, on every plan including Free, in open formats, including after a subscription has ended. Every number with its source, every document, every version, the whole history.

I bought the workbook. Does that count for anything?

Yes. Before They Ask carries a $147 credit, redeemable once against one annual subscription. It does not expire, and redeeming it carries your workbook answers into the product.
